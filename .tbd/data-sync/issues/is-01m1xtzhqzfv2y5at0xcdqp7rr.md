The distinctive idea. The reader is asked two questions and they are never merged:

  1. Who killed Fyodor Pavlovitch?
  2. Who is responsible for his death?

Answerable at any point, changeable at any point, and every answer is recorded with the reading position it was given at. Question 2 accepts more than one name, and should — that is the whole argument.

Why this works only for this book: the trial is not a device imposed on it, it is Books XI–XII, and the divergence between the legal answer and the moral one is Dostoyevsky's thesis. The court answers 1 with Dmitri and calls it an answer to 2. Zossima answers 2 with everyone. The reader gets to find out where they stand.

Risk to manage: this is gimmick-shaped if the evidence under it is thin. It must be built on real extracted evidence with citations, not on vibes.
